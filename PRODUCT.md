# Product

## Register

product

## Users

MusicAgent primarily serves everyday music enthusiasts who want to turn an idea into something they can hear quickly. It also supports professional musicians who need enough control to edit an arrangement, inspect its structure, and export it for continued work.

## Product Purpose

MusicAgent is a browser-based AI music workspace for generating, auditioning, editing, arranging, and exporting music without requiring Sonic Pi or another external playback application. Success means a first-time user can describe an idea and hear it quickly, while an experienced musician can inspect and refine the underlying notes, tracks, tempo, and mix before export.

## Brand Personality

Lively, concise, and youthful. The product should feel immediate and expressive without becoming noisy or toy-like. Its working surface should borrow Ableton Live's clarity, density, and direct manipulation rather than imitate its visual styling.

## Anti-references

- Marketing-style dashboards that hide the music workspace behind oversized promotional content.
- Decorative card grids, glass effects, and ambient gradients that compete with editing tasks.
- Toy-like music interfaces that remove timing, track, arrangement, and export control.
- Dense professional software that assumes prior production knowledge for basic generation and playback.
- Excessive animation or visualization that is disconnected from the audio signal.

## Design Principles

- Hear the result early: generation should lead directly to in-browser auditioning.
- Reveal control progressively: keep first use simple while retaining track and arrangement depth.
- Make time visible: playback position, note timing, waveform, and structure must stay synchronized.
- Prefer direct manipulation: editing notes, tracks, tempo, and mix should produce immediate feedback.
- Keep the workspace honest: status, errors, exports, and generated artifacts must reflect actual system state.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA. Maintain sufficient contrast, visible focus states, semantic controls, reduced-motion behavior, and accessible names for playback and editing tools. Keyboard-first operation and a dedicated color-blind mode are not current product requirements, but standard controls must remain operable with normal browser keyboard navigation. Motion should be limited to meaningful playback feedback and transitions that clarify state.
